COVID-19 effect: Millennials to go for homebuying

#Coronavirus #Coronavirus Columns #Investment #Property Punditz
3 min read Jun 10, 2021

By HouseThat

Real Estate Knowledge Platform

454 Views
1 Times shared
Share:

A year after the Coronavirus pandemic struck, millennials have emerged as the biggest home-buying segment. Why are they opting to buy homes now? And what do millennials want in a home? We break it down. Millennials know exactly what they want. And right now, in the COVID era, it is a home of their own.

Survey after survey has documented this changing paradigm. But, just why did this formerly 'no-strings-attached' generation choose to commit to the rigour of home loan EMIs? No other reason but plain old pragmatism and sound financial sense, or what the Millennials would call 'Adulting'.

The pandemic reality check

Up until now, millennials preferred to live on rent. This gave them the flexibility of location and budget with the opportunity to live among friends. The COVID-19 pandemic saw people trapped indoors for months on end. Work-from-home became the norm. Personal space came at a premium.

Millennials are now the biggest home-buying segment post-COVID-19

Caught in the turbulence of a worldwide pandemic, tanking economies and uncertain job markets, this generation newly appreciated the need to create physical assets to ensure a roof over their heads. Owning a home had suddenly become critical.

Financial reality check

The pandemic also saw the emergence of a Buyer's market in real estate. Stagnant property prices, historically low home loan interest rates and tax benefits made real estate a financially attractive option again.

How so? Take a look at the numbers:

  • Apartment Cost = Rs 1,00,00,000
  • Home Loan = Rs 80,00,000
  • Monthly EMI @ 6.75 percent for 30 Years = Rs 51,888
  • Tax deductions under 80C* and 24B** = Rs 21,949 per month
  • Total cost of Home (less tax savings) = Rs 29,939 per month

Consider a 28-year-old earning Rs 12 lakh per annum and paying Rs 35,000 monthly as rent for a 1 BHK flat. The money paid as rent is a pure expense. It merely enables the person to pay for a month's shelter.

This homeowner can now avail tax savings up to Rs 21,949 per month under section 80C and 24B (under the old tax regime). This brings the monthly expense (after taxes) down to Rs 29,939 per month, which is Rs 5,061 lesser than what the person would pay as rent. Add to this the equity he is building in an asset that is already worth Rs 1 crore. And if the homeowner is a woman, the tax benefits are even higher in some states. All of which makes immense sense to the financially savvy Millennial. Hence, the rush to buy a home.

But what is it that Millennials look for when they buy a home?

Millennials have grown up with digital devices that bundle communication, entertainment, shopping, and education. The world is truly, their oyster. Compared to their elders, they also have higher income levels and access to better infrastructure. All this means that this generation has a very evolved sense of who they are and what they want. This echoes in their homes which reflect their personalities.

The good life at a good price

Millennials are tight on budgets but they do not compromise on what matters to them. They prioritise location, amenities, good designs and finishes over the size of the apartment or the number of rooms.

Location

Location was one of the key reasons Millennials preferred to rent in pre-Covid times, and it is still a favoured criterion for a home purchase. Thus, you will find they are willing to shell out big bucks for homes that allow them to 'walk to work' or are located in prime residential locations near a commercial hub. Stagnant property prices and new supply have made such locations more affordable now.

Multifunction spaces

Multifunction spaces are In. Traditional 1/2/3 BHK houses and open-plan homes are out. Millennial buyers are demanding 'optimised' homes that cater to one's well-being, such as spaces for meditating, workout areas, expansive kitchens, and an eco-friendly environment.

Smart homes

Wi-fi enabled homes are now considered a basic amenity, and this generation expects homes powered by Alexa, Google or other home technology solutions. Many developers have already started offering home technology solutions in their projects.

Smart design

The home has become more than a mere living space in the Covid era. It is now a space where you also work, play, study and entertain. Not surprisingly, new homebuyers are looking for smartly designed layouts that are flexible as opposed to structured living, dining, waiting or storage areas.

Lifestyle amenities

Millennials are very socially active, and they like to keep their friends close. Hence, external amenities like a clubhouse, gym or a lawn to host parties are a big draw with this buyer segment.

Experiences

This is a generation that thrives on 'experiences'. Millennials covet experiences the way their parents coveted goods. Thus, you will see a preference for homes with quality fittings built by branded developers, a demand for transparency and a deep pride in homeownership.

To conclude, this is a homebuyer who knows his/her mind. They might appear fastidious and difficult to please, but that is because they are very serious about their dream home.

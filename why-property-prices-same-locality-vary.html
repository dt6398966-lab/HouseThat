Picture this: You've just shortlisted a project and find out your friend bought a flat in the neighbouring housing society, but paid Rs 15 lakh less. Same sector, same view, almost similar carpet areas- then why the price gap? It's natural that you now want to migrate to your friend's project. But before you do, hit pause and first understand what's actually driving this difference.

Factors that affect flat prices in the same locality

By HouseThat Editorial

Research Analyst, HouseThat

Turns out, a project's pricing isn't just about its location or perception- it's a strategic play. Homebuyers don't simply pay for four walls and a roof. Instead, they pay for the ecosystem a project builds or promises to offer.

The real estate market shows significant price variations even within the same locality

So what really makes one project pricier than the one right beside it? Let's unpack the seven key reasons.

Why do prices vary between projects in the same location?

From brand to build quality, let's peel back the layers to understand why the project next door comes with a higher or lower ticket size:

The builder's market equity
1

What are the top two things on your homebuying checklist? For many, it's usually the location and the builder's brand value. When you look for a brand that you can trust, you're essentially hoping for quality, smooth handover, and consistent post-sale assistance.

Thus, call it the "trust tax", but a reputed builder known for its clean track record, timely delivery, and solid resale value will easily command Rs 500 - 1000 per sq ft, if not more, compared to the other projects in the same sector.

Take Jaypee Aman and Ace Starlit, for example. Located within walking distances of each other, Jaypee Aman by Jaypee Infratech is priced at Rs 7,350 per sq ft, whereas Starlit by Ace Developers are currently trending at Rs 13,800/ sq ft. Same view, same locality, but with a Rs 6,450 per sq ft difference.

Construction quality and the material grade
2

Back in the early 2000s, construction of high-rise structures often took twice the time that it takes today. The problem was long construction tenure, and the solution came in the form of Mivan shuttering. First introduced in India by reputed builders Larson & Turbo, this construction method slashed the construction timeline by 30 to 50 percent.

What's more? This framework of refabricated aluminium forms started casting entire concrete blocks at once, rather than building brick by brick, which made the walls stronger and more durable in the process.

Apart from build quality, branded materials also play a role in determining the unit's ask price. For example, a builder offering Jaquar faucets in the kitchen and bathroom will any day ask for a higher per sq ft price, compared to one offering local fittings and fixtures.

Also Read: How to check the construction quality of your new home?

Density and layout efficiency
3

Two towers might face the same park, but one could have only four units per floor while the other has eight. A project's density or population has a lot to do with the pressure it puts on common infrastructure like lifts and clubhouses. Thus, the lower the density, the more exclusive the project is.

The project density can also be mapped by the total area the project covers as opposed to the number of units it offers. For instance, projects with 69 units per acre are broadly categorised as low-density, although this definition may vary based on the project type or location. Apart from that, layout plans with wider corridors, better ventilation, or larger balconies also play an essential role in determining their final price.

The upkeep of the ecosystem
4

A rooftop pool, co-working pods, and landscaped gardens sound great, but the question is: who's maintaining them? Projects with robust maintenance teams, gated security, and active Resident Welfare Associations (RWAs) tend to retain value better. Thus, when comparing projects, buyers are often willing to pay more for a lifestyle that actually works, not just looks good on brochures.

But here's the thing: A project that hosts a lot of amenities requires a larger maintenance team, which means you'll probably end up paying more for monthly maintenance. So, before being swept up in the upkeep of the ecosystem, do check whether the recurring monthly expenses are cutting into your budget.

The age of the property
5

A 10-year old housing society is more likely to have ash brick construction, leading to most structures struggling with everyday wear and tear. Older ready-to-move projects, in comparison to new launches, may also feel outdated in terms of amenities or design. For instance, double-heighted entry lobbies and infinity pools are currently trending, but you would hardly find an old housing society sporting these features.

Another major consideration here is the price appreciation potential of a project. Prices in older housing societies have already appreciated and hence, the scope for further growth is limited. On the other hand, the under-construction project next door, holds immense price appreciation potential.

Legal clarity and track record
6

A clean title holds much more power than a glossy brochure or a flashy launch. Buyers may get swayed by price drops, festive schemes, or marketing buzz. However, if the land title isn't clear or the project has a history of litigation, that "deal" can quickly turn into a long-term headache. Banks hesitate to finance such properties, resale becomes tricky, and possession timelines stretch endlessly.

In contrast, a project with all approvals in place, zero disputes, and a clean RERA record can command a premium, and rightly so. You're not just paying for the flat; you're paying for your peace of mind, future liquidity, and the ability to sleep easy knowing your investment won't get stuck.

Micro-location advantages like proximity to metro, schools, and markets affect pricing

Micro-location perks
7

Micro-location isn't about the sector or even the street, it's about the exact spot your building sits on. Two projects might face the same lake or park, but one could be right on the main road while the other sits 300 meters inside, down a narrower lane. One might have a smoother entry point, better approach road width, or easier access to daily conveniences like grocery stores, schools, or metro stations, even if both technically share the same pincode.

These subtle differences in positioning affect not just daily conveniences, but also buyer perception and resale potential. Developers factor this in while pricing. So, while the view may be identical, the living experience may drive a difference in the price tag.

Price differences aren't just numbers—they reveal what the market values most: certainty, convenience, and confidence. Sometimes paying more isn't about luxury, it's about avoiding hidden headaches. The smarter move? Look beyond walls and views; weigh what you're actually buying before signing on the dotted line.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Does a better layout really affect pricing that much?
A. In most cases, yes. Zero-wastage layouts, larger balconies, and corner units can command a premium even within the same project or tower.
Q. Is it worth paying more for a branded developer?
A. It's more about studying the developer's track record than just chasing a big name. Consistency, delivery, and post-handover support matter more than branding alone.
Q. What is loading factor and does it impact a project's pricing?
A. Loading factor refers to the ratio of common area to the saleable area. Lower loading factors mean you get more usable space for the same price, making the project more value-efficient. Projects with lower loading factors (typically 15-25%) are considered better value propositions, while higher loading can reduce the actual usable space you're paying for.
